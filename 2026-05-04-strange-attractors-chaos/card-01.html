The Discovery

1961: A Typo That Changed Everything

Edward Lorenz restarted his weather simulation from a printout. The numbers looked identical. But the weather pattern that emerged was nothing like the original. Two trajectories that started imperceptibly close diverged completely. He'd found the first strange attractor — by accident.

"Two trajectories that start imperceptibly close will diverge exponentially over time. After enough iterations, they might as well be on opposite sides of the attractor."
