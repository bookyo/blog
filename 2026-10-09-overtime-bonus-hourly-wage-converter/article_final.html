Why Three Regimes Return Three Different Answers for the Same Hours

US, China, and EU/UK all pay overtime, but the definition of "overtime", what counts toward the regular rate, and the cap on hours all diverge. The calculator makes those divergences explicit instead of hiding them in assumptions:

- US FLSA: 1.5x the regular rate for hours beyond 40 per workweek. The calendar day doesn't matter federally — Sunday-to-Saturday defines the workweek. Nondiscretionary bonuses raise the regular rate itself (DOL method: (straight-time + bonus) / hours worked). - China: Base hourly = monthly salary ÷ 21.75 ÷ 8. Weekday overtime = 150%, rest days = 200% (without compensatory rest), statutory holidays = 300%. Hard cap: 3 hours/day and 36 hours/month overtime. - EU/UK: No statutory overtime multiplier — the contractual one wins. The 48-hour average week (17-week UK reference, opt-out possible) is the only enforced limit. Night-shift premiums are employer policy, not statute.

What the Bonus Actually Does to the Regular Rate

This is the single most-misunderstood number in cross-border payroll. The US Department of Labor method says:

regular_rate = (straight_time_earnings + nondiscretionary_bonus) / hours_worked

If your employee earned $7,500 monthly plus a $5,000 annual bonus, and worked 2,080 regular hours plus 144 overtime hours in a year, the regular rate climbs by the bonus-per-hour, and the overtime premium applies to the new regular rate. China and EU/UK treat the bonus as a separate line — it doesn't enter the regular-rate calculation. The calculator's compliance-warning panel flags this divergence on every run so you don't miss it.

Compliance Warnings the Calculator Raises

Three warnings fire automatically when the input crosses a known legal boundary:

- US: weekly hours exceed 40 with no compensatory rest configured → "regular rate recalculated against 48.5-hour week". - China: overtime total exceeds 36 hours/month or any single day exceeds 3 hours → "statutory cap exceeded; hours above cap excluded from pay". - EU/UK: 17-week rolling average above 48 hours and no opt-out recorded → "Working Time Directive breach; either record opt-out or reduce schedule".

The warnings are not advisory — they map directly to each jurisdiction's enforcement triggers. See the calculator for the exact threshold logic.

When Each Regime Wins (and Loses)

- US wins on simplicity when you have a clean 40-hour week and a small bonus — the FLSA math is one formula and audit-friendly. - China wins on transparency for shift workers — the 21.75 divisor is publicly posted and every employee can recompute it. - EU/UK wins on flexibility for high-skill contractors — the contractual multiplier and opt-out cover cases the US/China frameworks simply don't address.
