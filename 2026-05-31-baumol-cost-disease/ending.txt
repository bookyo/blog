The same logic that makes a concert tonight more expensive than a television made in 1960 is quietly reshaping every budget you manage — from your city's police department to your own company's payroll. Baumol's cost disease doesn't have a cure, because it isn't a disease at all. It's a structural feature of how productivity works in different sectors. Once you see it, you can't unsee it: the progressive sector drives genuine material prosperity, while the stagnant sector absorbs the rising cost of human attention, care, and performance — the things that can't be automated away. Understanding which sector your work belongs to is the first step toward making better decisions about pricing, investing, and career choice.
