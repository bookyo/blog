The Accessible Color Palette Contrast Checker lives at [Elysia Tools](https://elysiatools.com/en/tools/accessible-color-palette-contrast-checker) — it walks every color pair in your palette against WCAG 2.1 AA and AAA thresholds and shows you, in a single table, which pairings will fail for a low-vision reader on a sunny phone screen. You paste hex codes in, you get back a matrix with pass/fail markers for body text, large text, and UI components. It is not glamorous work, but it is the kind of work that decides whether 253 million people across the world — the WHO's most recent estimate of people living with visual impairment — can read your site, or close it. The math behind WCAG contrast is older than the web. The relative luminance formula was first published in 1994, four years before Google was incorporated. The reason the rule is still in force thirty years later is that the underlying visual science has not changed: human eyes separate the rods and cones into two channels, and the loss of contrast is the loss of information. The Elysia Tools checker is the practical front end for that science. It does not argue with the formula, it does not offer a creative reinterpretation, and it does not let you design your way out of a 3.2:1 ratio. It just shows you the truth, pair by pair, so you can fix what is broken before the next person with low vision tries to read your pricing page and gives up.
