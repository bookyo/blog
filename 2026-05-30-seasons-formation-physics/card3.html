The Photon Flux Argument

At 45° latitude in summer, solar elevation is ~68.5° — rays hit at ~21.5° from horizontal, giving cosine ≈ 0.93. In winter, solar elevation drops to ~21.5° — rays hit at ~68.5° from horizontal, cosine ≈ 0.37. Summer delivers 2.5× more energy per square meter than winter. That is why mid-latitude seasons are extreme.

The same energy, same distance — only the angle changes.
